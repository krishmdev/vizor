Does Higher Tire Pressure Roll Faster

We rode a power meter equipped commuter bike with 32 mm tires over two 1 km test sections, at 30 km/h, repeating each pressure four times.

Smooth asphalt

On new asphalt, rolling resistance fell as pressure rose, but slowly. Going from 50 to 90 psi saved only 3 W.

Rough chip seal

On rough road, the result flipped. At 55 psi the bike needed 16 W less than at 90 psi, because a very hard tire bounces the bike and rider over each bump instead of absorbing it. Above about 65 psi, every extra 10 psi cost 4 to 6 W.

What we recommend

For a 75 to 80 kg rider on 32 mm tires, 55 to 60 psi was the fastest range on typical city roads, and it felt far less harsh. Our testers rated 90 psi as the least comfortable pressure on every section.

Pressure loss

Our tires with standard butyl tubes lost 8 psi in seven days, and those with lightweight latex tubes lost 21 psi. Check weekly.

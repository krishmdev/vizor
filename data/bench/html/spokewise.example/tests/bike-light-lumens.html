Front Bike Lights Tested: Measured Lumens and Real Runtime

We measured six rechargeable front lights in an integrating sphere at 20 °C, then ran each on its highest mode until it shut off or dropped below 10 percent output.

Results

The Larkspur Beam 800 ($59) measured 780 lumens, 97 percent of its claim, and ran 1 hour 34 minutes on high. Its cut-off beam put the most light on the road of any light under $100.

The Nightjar 1000 ($89) measured 870 lumens but stepped down to half power after 20 minutes and lasted 58 minutes in total. The Cog & Chain Glow 600 measured 540 lumens and ran 2 hours 10 minutes. The cheapest light we bought, a no-name 1200 lumen model, measured 310.

Cold weather

At 0 °C, runtime on high fell by 18 to 30 percent across all six lights.

How many lumens you need

In our road tests, 300 to 400 measured lumens was enough to be seen on lit streets. On an unlit path at 25 km/h, riders spotted a pothole 12 m sooner with 780 lumens than with 400.

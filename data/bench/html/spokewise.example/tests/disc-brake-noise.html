Resin vs Sintered Disc Brake Pads, Measured

We fitted two identical commuter bikes with resin and sintered pads, rode them for 1,200 km through a wet spring, and measured noise at 1 m from the rotor during stops from 25 km/h.

Noise

Dry, resin pads peaked at 62 dB and sintered pads at 71 dB. Wet, both got louder: resin reached 84 dB and sintered 92 dB for the first two or three stops, then dropped by about 15 dB once the rotor was wiped clean by the pads.

Stopping

Dry stopping distance from 25 km/h was within 0.2 m for both. In the wet, sintered pads stopped 0.6 m shorter on the first stop.

Wear

After 1,200 km the resin pads had worn 1.9 mm and the sintered pads 0.8 mm.

Our take

For most commuters, resin pads are the better choice in dry climates because they are quieter. In a wet city, sintered pads last more than twice as long, and the extra noise is mostly the first few stops.

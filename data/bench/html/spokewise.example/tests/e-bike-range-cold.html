How Much Range Do E-Bikes Lose in the Cold

We rode three commuter e-bikes, including the Larkspur Volt E1 and the Cog & Chain Current E, on the same 22 km loop at three temperatures, with batteries stored outdoors overnight before each run.

Range at each temperature

At 20 °C the Volt E1 went 58 km and the Current E 71 km. At 5 °C both lost about 12 percent. At -5 °C the Volt E1 managed 39 km, 33 percent less, and the Current E 47 km, 34 percent less.

Warm battery, cold ride

When we stored the batteries indoors at 20 °C and fitted them just before a -5 °C ride, the loss was only 14 to 16 percent. Where you keep the battery overnight matters more than anything else. A neoprene cover saved another 3 percent on the -5 °C ride. All runs used the middle assist level and a 78 kg rider.

80 vs 100 percent

In our 18 month cycling test of identical cells, those charged to 100 percent every day had 83 percent capacity left after 600 cycles. Those charged to 80 percent had 92 percent.

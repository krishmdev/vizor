The Best Gravel Bikes Under $2,000, Tested

We rode five gravel bikes for a combined 800 km on commutes, dirt roads and a gravel loop, and measured each frame's real tire clearance with calipers.

Best value: Larkspur Gravel GX

At $1,399 the Larkspur Gravel GX was the cheapest bike in the test and the one we would buy for a mixed commute. Our size 54 weighed 9.9 kg without pedals. We measured 6 mm of space on each side of the stock 40 mm tires, which is enough for a 45 mm tire or a fender, not both. The 46/30 crankset gave the lowest climbing gear in the group.

Best on rough gravel: Ferrow Terra

The Ferrow Terra ($1,899) cleared 50 mm tires and was the most stable bike on loose descents. It weighed 10.4 kg.

Lightest: Brisa Rambler

The Brisa Rambler ($1,749) weighed 9.3 kg but only cleared 38 mm tires, and it has no fender mounts, so it is a poor commuter.

How much clearance you need

For commuting on pavement and packed paths, 35 to 40 mm tires are plenty. For loose gravel, aim for 42 to 45 mm and leave at least 4 mm of room each side for mud.

Saddle Height Methods Compared

We set saddle height for 20 riders three ways: the heel method, the 109 percent inseam formula, and a goniometer targeting a 30 degree knee angle at the bottom of the stroke.

How far apart they were

For most riders, the three methods landed within 10 mm of each other. The largest gap was 22 mm. The heel method set the saddle an average of 6 mm lower than the goniometer, mostly because riders with thick-soled shoes got a lower result.

Which to use

The 109 percent method was the most repeatable: two testers measuring the same rider agreed within 3 mm. The heel method varied by up to 12 mm between testers. We recommend 109 percent as a starting point, then a check with the heel method.

Knee pain

Of our riders, 7 had knee pain at their previous height. Five were more than 15 mm too low and had front of knee pain. Two were too high and had pain behind the knee. All seven improved within three weeks after adjustment.

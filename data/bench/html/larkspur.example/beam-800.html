Larkspur Beam 800

The Larkspur Beam 800 is a rechargeable front light for commuting on dark roads, unlit bike paths and busy city streets.

A beam that lights the road

Most small bike lights throw a round spot of light that also shines straight into the eyes of oncoming riders and drivers. The Beam 800 has a shaped lens with a flat top edge, similar to a car headlight, so the light lands on the road ahead instead of in people's faces.

How much light you need

On lit city streets, the job of a front light is mostly to be seen, and 200 to 400 lumens is plenty. On unlit paths and country roads you need to see potholes, gravel and animals early, and that is where 600 to 800 lumens makes a real difference at commuting speeds.

A side window on each side of the lens adds visibility at junctions.

Battery life

Runtime drops quickly on the highest mode, and it drops further in cold weather because lithium cells deliver less capacity below about 5 °C. If your light dies early, use a lower mode on lit streets, keep the light indoors between rides in winter, and charge it fully before long rides. A battery that has been stored empty for months can also lose capacity for good.

Specifications

The Beam 800 has four modes: 800, 400 and 200 lumens, plus a daytime flash. Rated runtime is 1 hour 30 minutes on 800, 3 hours on 400, 6 hours on 200 and 24 hours on flash. It charges by USB-C in about 3 hours. It is rated IP64 for rain and weighs 118 g with the mount.

The Larkspur Beam 800 costs $59.

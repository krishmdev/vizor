Preventing Bike Theft

Most bike thefts are quick and opportunistic. A thief looks for the easiest bike on the street, so the goal is to make yours harder to take than the one next to it. This Larkspur guide covers locking, parking and what to do if your bike is stolen.

How to lock a bike

Lock the frame, not just a wheel, to something solid that cannot be cut or lifted over, such as a fixed bike rack or a thick metal post. Put the U-lock around the frame and the rear wheel inside the rear triangle. Keep the lock off the ground, where it can be smashed, and fill the inside of the lock so there is no room for a lever. Add a cable or a second lock for the front wheel.

Where to park

Busy, well-lit spots with foot traffic and cameras are safer than quiet side streets. Never leave a bike locked outside overnight if you can avoid it.

If your bike is stolen

Report it to the police straight away, with the serial number, photos and a receipt. Register it as stolen on bike registries, tell your insurer, and check local online marketplaces and second-hand shops for the next few weeks.

How much to spend

A common rule is to spend about 10 to 20 percent of the bike's value on locks. Write down your serial number today; it is stamped under the bottom bracket on every Larkspur bike.

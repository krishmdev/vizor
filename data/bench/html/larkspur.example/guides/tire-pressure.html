Tire Pressure

Getting tire pressure right is the cheapest upgrade you can make to any bike. This Larkspur guide explains how to choose a pressure and how to tell when it is wrong.

Wider tires need less pressure

The number printed on the sidewall is a safe range, not a target. Wider tires hold the same load at lower pressure, and your weight matters as much as the tire size. Heavier riders and riders carrying a loaded pannier should add pressure.

Signs your pressure is wrong

If the bike feels harsh, skips over bumps and loses grip on rough corners, the pressure is too high. If it feels sluggish, squirms in corners, or you get pinch flats with two small holes side by side, the pressure is too low. Many riders assume harder tires are faster, but on real roads a slightly softer tire absorbs bumps instead of bouncing off them, and ends up rolling faster.

Check it weekly

Tubes lose air through the rubber, so a tire can drop 5 to 10 psi in a week without a puncture. Check with a floor pump gauge before your first ride each week.

Starting pressures

For a 75 kg rider, try these and adjust in steps of 5 psi. On 28 mm tires, 70 psi front and 75 psi rear. On 32 mm tires, 55 psi front and 60 psi rear. On 40 mm tires, such as those on the Larkspur Metro 7 and Gravel GX, 40 psi front and 45 psi rear. Add about 2 psi for every extra 5 kg of rider and luggage. The Larkspur Floor Pump reads accurately enough for this, and fills a 28 mm tire in 18 strokes.

Saddle Height and Knee Pain

Saddle height is the most important fit adjustment on any bike. A saddle that is too low or too high is the most common cause of knee pain in new riders. This Larkspur guide explains how to set it.

Setting saddle height

With your cycling shoes on, sit on the saddle and put your heel on the pedal at the bottom of the stroke. Your leg should be straight. When you pedal normally, with the ball of your foot on the pedal, your knee will then have a slight bend at the bottom.

Knee pain

Pain at the front of the knee, around or under the kneecap, usually means the saddle is too low, so the knee stays too bent under load. Pain at the back of the knee usually means it is too high and you are overextending. Pain on the inside or outside of the knee is more often caused by foot position or cleat angle. Raise or lower the saddle in small steps of 3 to 5 mm and ride a few days between changes.

Other methods

The heel method is quick but depends on shoe thickness. An inseam formula is more repeatable.

Numbers

For the inseam method, multiply your inseam by 1.09 and measure that distance from the pedal axle, with the crank in line with the seat tube, to the top of the saddle. A second common formula is inseam times 0.883, measured from the center of the bottom bracket. At the bottom of the stroke, a knee angle of 25 to 35 degrees is the usual target. Larkspur bikes have seatpost height marks every 5 mm.

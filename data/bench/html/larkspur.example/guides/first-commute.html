Your First Bike Commute

Commuting by bike for the first time can feel like a big step. Most of the worry disappears after a week. This Larkspur guide covers the route, the gear and how to arrive fresh.

Plan the route

The fastest route by car is rarely the best by bike. Use a cycling map to find quieter streets and bike lanes, even if they add a few minutes. Ride the route on a weekend first so you know where the tricky junctions are.

Start small

You do not need to ride every day. Start with one or two days a week, or ride in and take transit home. Leave ten minutes earlier than you think you need.

Not arriving sweaty

Ride slower than feels natural, especially for the last kilometre, and let your body cool down before you arrive. Wear a light layer and carry your work clothes in a pannier instead of wearing a backpack. If your office has no shower, a pack of wipes, a small towel and a spare shirt go a long way. An e-bike removes most of the effort if your commute is long or hilly.

The gear you need

You need a bike in good working order, a helmet, front and rear lights, a good lock, and a way to carry things. Fenders are worth fitting if it rains where you live. A basic flat repair kit covers most problems on the road.

What it costs

If you already have a bike, the basics, a helmet, lights and a lock, cost around $150 to $250. For example, the Larkspur Shell helmet is $89, the Beam 800 light $59 and the Lock D9 $79. Most commutes of 5 to 10 km take 20 to 35 minutes by bike.

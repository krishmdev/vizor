Winter Commuting

Riding through the winter is easier than most people expect, once you have the right clothes, lights and a bit of extra maintenance. This Larkspur guide covers the basics.

Dressing for the cold

Dress in layers you can open or remove. You warm up fast on a bike, so start the ride feeling slightly cold. A thin base layer, a windproof jacket and a buff or neck warmer cover most days. Hands and feet get cold first, so wear windproof gloves or lobster mitts and wool socks. Below freezing, add a thin hat under your helmet and waterproof overshoes.

Tires and traction

Wider tires at slightly lower pressure grip better on wet and slushy roads. If your route has ice that does not get cleared, studded tires are worth buying. They grip on ice where normal tires simply slide, and they are the most effective single upgrade for icy commutes.

Road salt

Salt and grit eat chains, cassettes and brake parts. After salty rides, rinse the drivetrain and brakes with plain water, dry the chain and relube it with a wet lube. Check disc pads more often; they wear much faster in winter grit.

Lights

Use a bright front light and a rear light, charged every night.

Winter numbers

Studded tires cost about $60 to $120 each and are noticeably slower on dry roads, so run them at 30 to 40 psi only for the icy months. Rinse after every salty ride and lube the chain at least twice a week. The Larkspur Beam 800 runs 1 hour 30 minutes on its highest mode, but expect 20 to 30 percent less below freezing. The Larkspur Metro 7 internal hub is sealed against salt.

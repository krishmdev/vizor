Choosing a Frame Size

A bike that fits is safer, more comfortable and more fun to ride. This Larkspur guide explains how to choose a frame size, what to do when you fall between two sizes, and how to tell if your current bike is too big.

Height and inseam

Your height gives a starting point, but leg length matters more for standover and saddle height, and torso and arm length matter more for reach. Measure your inseam standing barefoot against a wall with a book pressed up between your legs.

Reach

Reach is how far forward the handlebar is from the saddle. If your back aches, your hands go numb, or your arms are locked straight, the bike is probably too stretched out for you. A shorter stem or a handlebar with more backsweep can help, but a frame that is much too long is hard to fix.

Between sizes

If you are between two sizes, choose based on how you ride. The smaller frame is more nimble and easier to make longer with a longer stem. The larger frame is more stable and upright. For commuting, most riders between sizes are happier on the smaller frame.

Size chart

For the Larkspur Metro 7, small fits riders 155 to 168 cm, medium 168 to 182 cm and large 182 to 195 cm. For the Gravel GX, size 50 fits 158 to 167 cm, 52 fits 165 to 173 cm, 54 fits 171 to 179 cm, 56 fits 177 to 185 cm and 58 fits 183 to 192 cm. You should have at least 2 cm of standover clearance.

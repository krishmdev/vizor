Fixing a Flat Tire

Every rider gets a flat tire sooner or later. This Larkspur guide covers how to fix one at the side of the road and how to stop it from happening again.

Fixing a flat on the road

Shift to the smallest rear cog, open the brake if it has a quick release, and remove the wheel. Push the tire bead into the center of the rim all the way around, then use tire levers to lift one side of the tire off. Pull out the tube.

Before fitting a new tube, run your fingers carefully around the inside of the tire to find whatever caused the flat. If you skip this, the same thorn or glass will puncture the new tube within minutes. Put a little air into the new tube so it holds its shape, tuck it in, and roll the tire back on with your thumbs.

Why you keep getting flats

Repeated flats almost always come from one of four causes: tire pressure that is too low, which lets the tube pinch against the rim; something still stuck in the tire; worn tires with thin tread; or rim tape that has slipped and exposes a spoke hole. Check pressure weekly and replace worn tires.

What to carry

A basic kit is a spare tube, two tire levers, a patch kit and a mini pump or CO2 inflator.

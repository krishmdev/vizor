Cleaning and Lubing a Chain

A clean, lubed chain shifts better, runs quieter and lasts much longer. This Larkspur guide covers a quick routine that takes about ten minutes.

Cleaning

Wipe the chain with a rag while turning the pedals backward. If the chain is black and gritty, use a chain cleaning tool or a brush with a bike-specific degreaser, then rinse with water and let it dry fully.

Lubing

Put one drop of lube on each roller on the inside of the chain while turning the pedals slowly. Turn the cranks for a minute so the lube works into the links, then wipe the outside of the chain firmly with a clean rag. The lube needs to be inside the rollers, not on the outside where it collects dirt.

Wet or dry lube

Wet lube is thicker and stays on in rain, but picks up more grime. Dry lube stays cleaner but washes off quickly in wet weather. Many commuters use wet lube from autumn to spring and dry lube in summer.

Squeaks after rain

A chain that squeaks after a wet ride has had its lube washed out. Wipe it dry and lube it again the same day, or rust will start on the rollers.

How often

For daily commuting, lube the chain every 150 to 300 km, or after every ride in heavy rain. Do a full clean about once a month. Check chain wear with a gauge every 1,000 km and replace the chain at 0.5 percent stretch on 11 and 12-speed drivetrains, or 0.75 percent on others. The Larkspur Metro 7 chainguard keeps the chain cleaner, so it can go about twice as long between cleans.

Squealing Disc Brakes

Disc brakes can squeal, especially in the rain. This Larkspur support page explains the common causes and fixes.

Why disc brakes squeal

Squeal is a vibration between the pad and the rotor. The most common causes are water, contamination and pads that were never bedded in. Rain and road spray put a film of grit and water on the rotor, and the brakes usually sing for the first few stops until the pads clean it off. That kind of noise is normal and goes away as the rotor dries.

Squealing that continues in dry weather usually means oil or grease has reached the pads or rotor, from chain lube, a bike cleaning spray or a fingerprint. Clean the rotor with isopropyl alcohol. Contaminated pads should be replaced.

Bedding in new pads

New pads need to be bedded in to lay down an even layer of pad material on the rotor. Find a safe flat road, ride at about 20 km/h and brake firmly to walking pace, without locking the wheel. Repeat 10 to 20 times on each brake. Do not come to a complete stop with the brake held on while the rotor is hot.

Pad types

Resin pads are quieter and bite well from cold, but wear faster, especially in wet grit. Sintered metal pads last longer and cope better with heat and rain, but are noisier.

Our pads

Larkspur bikes such as the Metro 7 and Volt E1 ship with resin pads. In wet commuting, resin pads last about 1,000 to 1,500 km and sintered pads 2,000 to 3,000 km. Replace pads when the pad material is thinner than 1 mm. Larkspur replacement pads cost $18 a pair.

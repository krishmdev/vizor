E-Bike Battery Care and Range

The battery is the most expensive part of an e-bike to replace, and how you charge and store it has a big effect on how long it lasts. This Larkspur support page applies to the Volt E1 and the Haul.

Everyday charging

Lithium-ion cells age fastest when they sit at a full charge or completely empty, and when they are hot. For daily commuting, charging to about 80 percent and recharging before the battery drops below 20 percent is gentler than charging to 100 percent every night. Charge to 100 percent when you need the full range for a long ride, and start riding soon after it finishes. Always charge at room temperature, never below freezing.

Cold weather range

Cold batteries cannot deliver their full capacity, so range drops in winter even though the battery is not damaged. Store and charge the battery indoors and fit it just before you leave. The range comes back when temperatures rise.

Storing over winter

If you will not ride for more than a month, store the battery off the bike, indoors, at 40 to 60 percent charge, somewhere between 10 and 20 °C. Check it every two months and top it back up to about half. Never store it empty; a deeply discharged battery may not wake up again.

Numbers to know

Expect 20 to 30 percent less range near 0 °C than at 20 °C. A Larkspur battery keeps about 80 percent of its capacity after 800 full charge cycles if charged to 100 percent every time, and after about 1,200 cycles if usually charged to 80 percent. The Volt E1 charger has an 80 percent setting on its switch. A replacement 504 Wh Volt E1 battery costs $599.

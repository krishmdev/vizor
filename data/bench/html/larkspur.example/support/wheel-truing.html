Wobbly Wheels and Truing

A wheel that wobbles from side to side is out of true. It usually happens after a pothole, a curb or a fall, as spokes loosen or bend the rim slightly. This Larkspur support page explains how to check it and how to make small repairs at home.

Checking the wheel

Lift the bike and spin the wheel slowly. Watch the gap between the rim and the brake pads, or a fixed point such as a zip tie on the frame. A wheel that moves sideways is out of true. First check that the wheel is properly seated in the dropouts and the axle is tight, because a loose wheel can look like a buckled one.

Truing at home

You need a spoke key in the right size. Find the part of the rim that moves toward the rub. Loosen the spokes on that side by a quarter turn, and tighten the spokes on the opposite side by a quarter turn, working on two or three spokes around the wobble. Spin, check and repeat in small steps. Squeeze pairs of spokes to feel for any that are much looser than their neighbors.

When to stop

If the rim is dented, cracked or badly bent, or several spokes are broken, a shop will need to repair or replace the wheel.

Tolerances and costs

Aim for less than 1 mm of side-to-side movement. On disc brake bikes, rubbing is often the rotor, not the rim; a bent rotor can be straightened by hand. A spoke key costs about $8. Shops typically charge $20 to $40 to true a wheel. A replacement rear wheel for the Larkspur Metro 7 costs $139.

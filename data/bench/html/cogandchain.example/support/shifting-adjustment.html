How to Adjust Your Gears

Most shifting problems on a commuter bike can be fixed in five minutes with no tools, using the barrel adjuster.

The quick fix

Put the bike in the middle of the cassette. Turn the pedals and listen. A ticking or rattling chain means the derailleur is sitting between two cogs. Turn the barrel adjuster half a turn in one direction; if the noise gets worse, go back the other way. Stop when the chain runs quietly and shifts crisply both up and down. If you have to turn the adjuster more than two full turns, the cable may be sticking in its housing. New cable and housing cost $25 in parts at our stores.

If adjustment does not work

Look at the derailleur from behind. If it is not in a straight line with the cogs, the hanger is bent. A slipping chain under load, even with good adjustment, is a sign of chain and cassette wear.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my gears skip when I pedal hard?

Usually a worn chain and cassette. If the chain has stretched past 0.5 percent, replace both. Out of adjustment gears tend to skip whether you pedal hard or not.

How often should gears be adjusted?

After the first month on a new bike, then whenever shifting gets noisy. Most commuters need a small tweak every few months.

Can a bent hanger cause skipping?

Yes. Bikes that fall on their drive side often bend the hanger. A shop can straighten or replace it.

Cog & Chain Transit 8

The Transit 8 is a light, ready-to-ride city bike for commutes of 5 to 20 km.

Why the Transit 8

We built the Transit 8 around an 8-speed derailleur drivetrain. A derailleur gives a wider gear range than most hub gears, so the Transit 8 climbs steep streets more easily, and it keeps the bike light. The frame, fork and fenders together keep the weight at 12.6 kg, light enough to carry up a flight of stairs to an apartment.

The rack and fender mounts are threaded into the frame rather than clamped on, so nothing rattles loose over cobbles. The rear rack takes a pair of panniers, and the kickstand bolts to a plate behind the bottom bracket so it cannot crush the chainstay.

Mechanical disc brakes are simple to service at any shop. The 700 x 38c tires roll quickly on pavement and still handle broken asphalt and gravel paths.

Specifications

Aluminum frame and steel fork. 8-speed derailleur, 11-34 cassette. Mechanical disc brakes with 160 mm rotors. 700 x 38c tires. Fenders and a 20 kg rear rack. Weight 12.6 kg (size M). Price $799 with a five-year frame warranty.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Transit 8 good for hilly cities?

Yes. The 11-34 cassette gives low gears for steep climbs, which is the main reason we chose a derailleur over a hub gear for this bike.

How much maintenance does a derailleur need?

Plan on a quick gear adjustment after the first month as the cable stretches, then a clean and lube every few weeks. A shop tune-up once a year keeps shifting sharp.

What size Transit 8 should I buy?

Riders from 160 to 172 cm usually fit a small, 172 to 184 cm a medium and 184 to 196 cm a large.

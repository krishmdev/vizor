How to Fix a Flat Bike Tire

A flat is a ten minute job once you have done it twice. Here is the method our mechanics teach at every Cog & Chain store.

Step by step

Take the wheel off and let out any air left in the tube. Hook a lever under the tire bead opposite the valve, lever it over the rim, then slide a second lever around to free one side. Remove the tube, starting away from the valve.

Pump a little air into the old tube and listen for the hiss. Match the hole to the tire so you know where to look for the cause. Remove the object, then patch the tube or fit a new one, starting at the valve. Inflate slowly and check the tube is not trapped under the bead.

Patch or replace

A glueless patch gets you home. A glued patch, done properly, is as reliable as a new tube. Carrying a spare tube is faster on a cold day.

Frequently asked questions

What should I carry in a flat repair kit?

A spare tube in the right size, two plastic tire levers, a patch kit and a pump or CO2 inflator with two cartridges. Our commuter kit costs $29.

Why do I keep getting flats?

The most common causes are low pressure, a thorn or wire still in the tire, and worn tires. Two small holes side by side, a pinch flat, mean your pressure was too low.

Should I switch to tubeless?

Tubeless tires seal most small punctures on their own, which suits commuters who ride through glass. They need sealant topped up every three to six months.

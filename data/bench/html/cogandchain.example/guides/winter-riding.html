Winter Bike Commuting

Our staff ride all winter, and most of the problems they see come down to clothing, traction and salt.

What to wear

Wind is the enemy more than cold. A windproof shell over one warm mid-layer is enough down to about -5 °C for a 30 minute ride. Protect your ears, fingers and toes. Clear glasses keep freezing wind and grit out of your eyes, and a thin cap under the helmet covers the ears without needing a bigger helmet size. Heavy ski gloves are too bulky for shifting; wind-blocking cycling gloves with a thin liner work better.

Traction

Normal tires are fine on wet or cleared roads. On black ice or packed snow, studded tires are the only tire that grips. A pair of studded commuter tires costs around $140 and lasts several winters.

Salt

Salt water corrodes chains within days. Wipe and relube after salty rides and use a thicker wet lube. Full fenders keep the worst spray off you and the drivetrain.

Frequently asked questions

Are studded bike tires worth it?

If you ride on ice regularly, yes. On dry or wet roads they are slower and noisy, so fit them only when ice is likely.

How do I stop salt ruining my bike?

Rinse with plain water after salty rides, dry the chain and lube it again. A wax or wet lube holds up better than dry lube.

Can I ride an e-bike in winter?

Yes. Keep the battery indoors between rides and expect less range.

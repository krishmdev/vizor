How to Clean and Lube a Bike Chain

A dirty chain can wear out a cassette and chainrings in a single winter. Keeping it clean is the cheapest maintenance you can do.

The ten minute routine

Wipe the chain with a dry rag. Scrub it with a brush and a little citrus degreaser, then rinse gently and dry it with a clean rag. Apply lube slowly to the inner side of the chain, one drop per link, spin the cranks for 30 seconds and wipe off the excess.

Choosing a lube

We stock three types. Wet lube for rain and winter. Dry lube for dry, dusty summer riding. Wax, which lasts longest and stays cleanest but has to be applied to a fully clean chain. Whatever you use, apply less than you think. Excess lube on the outside of the chain picks up grit and turns into a grinding paste.

When to replace the chain

Chains stretch as the pins wear. Replace a chain when a wear gauge reads 0.5 percent to protect your cassette. Most commuter chains last 2,000 to 4,000 km.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I lube my bike chain?

Every 200 km or so, and after any ride in heavy rain. If the chain sounds dry or squeaks, it needs lube now.

Wet lube or dry lube for commuting?

If you ride through a wet winter, use wet lube. In dry weather, dry lube or wax keeps the chain cleaner.

Why does my chain squeak after it rains?

Rain washes the lube out of the rollers. Dry the chain and relube it the same day.

Fixing Flats on the Roadside Without Losing Your Mind

I had eleven flats in my first year of commuting. Last year I had two. Here is what changed.

The habit that fixed it

After every flat I now run my fingers around the inside of the tire before putting in the new tube. The first year, I kept getting a second flat a few kilometres later, because the tiny piece of wire was still there. Go slowly, it can cut you.

What I carry

One tube, two levers, a small patch kit, and a mini pump. I stopped carrying CO2 after a cartridge froze my fingers in January. The whole kit fits in a saddle bag and cost about $30.

My roadside routine

Flip the bike, wheel out, one lever, then the second, tube out. I put a bit of air in the new tube before fitting it so it doesn't twist. I pump to about 50 psi on the road, then top up properly with the floor pump at home.

The other big change was pumping my tires every Sunday. Most of my early flats were pinch flats from riding soft tires.

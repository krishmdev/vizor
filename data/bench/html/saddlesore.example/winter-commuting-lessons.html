Winter Commuting Lessons

I've commuted through four winters now, 12 km each way. Here's what actually matters.

Clothes

I start cold. If I'm warm standing outside my door, I'll be soaked with sweat after 10 minutes. At -10 °C I wear a merino base layer, a fleece, a windproof jacket, lobster mitts and a thin cap under my helmet. My feet were always the problem until I got neoprene overshoes.

Studded tires

I fell twice on black ice in my first winter. Studded tires cost me $110 for the pair and I haven't fallen since. They buzz on dry asphalt and feel slow, so they go on in December and come off in March.

Salt

My second winter I ignored the salt and by March my chain was orange and my brake pads were gone. Now I rinse the bike with a watering can when I get home and relube the chain twice a week.

Single Boiler vs Dual Boiler Espresso Machines

The main difference between single and dual boiler machines is whether you can brew and steam at the same time.

Single boiler

A single boiler machine uses one boiler for both jobs. You brew at around 93 °C, then switch to steam and wait while the boiler climbs to about 125 °C. With a PID, brew temperature is stable. The trade-off is the wait between brewing and steaming, usually under a minute.

Dual boiler

A dual boiler machine keeps a brew boiler and a steam boiler at their own temperatures. There is no waiting and no temperature surfing, which makes it faster for milk drinks. Dual boilers cost more, take longer to heat up from cold, and use more counter space and power.

Which should you choose

Choose a single boiler if you mostly drink espresso or make one milk drink at a time. Choose a dual boiler if you regularly make several lattes or cappuccinos back to back. The Aria and Aria Dual share the same group and brew quality.

Frequently asked questions

Is a dual boiler worth the extra cost?

It is worth it if you make more than two milk drinks in a row or want to brew and steam at the same time. For straight espresso, a single boiler with PID is enough.

What is a dual boiler espresso machine?

A dual boiler machine has one boiler for brewing and a separate boiler for steam, each held at its own temperature, so both are ready at once.

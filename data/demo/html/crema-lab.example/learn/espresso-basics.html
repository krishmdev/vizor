Espresso Basics: Dose, Grind, Time and Ratio

Good espresso comes from controlling four things: dose, grind, time and ratio. This guide explains each one.

Dose and ratio

Dose is the weight of dry coffee in the basket. Ratio is the weight of espresso in the cup compared with the dose. A 1:2 ratio, such as 18 grams in and 36 grams out, is the usual starting point. Shorter ratios taste heavier and more intense. Longer ratios taste lighter and can bring out more sweetness in light roasts.

Grind and time

Grind size controls how fast water moves through the coffee. Finer grinds slow the shot and extract more. Coarser grinds speed it up and extract less. Adjust grind until the shot hits your ratio in 25 to 30 seconds, then fine-tune by taste.

Water and temperature

Brew water between 90 and 96 °C works for most coffees. Soft to moderately hard water, around 50 to 100 ppm, extracts well and protects the machine from scale.

Frequently asked questions

How much coffee should I use for a double shot?

Use 18 to 20 grams of ground coffee in a double basket and aim for roughly twice that weight in the cup.

How long should an espresso shot take?

Most shots taste balanced between 25 and 30 seconds from the start of the pump. Use time as a guide and taste as the judge.

What grind size is right for espresso?

Espresso needs a fine grind, finer than table salt. The right setting is the one that gives your target ratio in about 25 to 30 seconds.

What does pre-infusion do?

Pre-infusion wets the coffee at low pressure for a few seconds before full pressure. It helps the puck saturate evenly and reduces channeling.

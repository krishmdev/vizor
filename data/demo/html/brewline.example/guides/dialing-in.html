Dialing in espresso

Dialing in means adjusting your recipe until a coffee tastes the way it should. This is the process we use in the Brewline test kitchen.

Start with a recipe

Pick a dose and a ratio and keep them fixed while you change the grind. A good starting point for a double basket is 18 grams of coffee in and 36 grams of liquid espresso out, a 1:2 ratio. Weigh both. Guessing by volume hides what is actually happening.

Change one thing at a time

Pull a shot and time it from when the pump starts. If it runs faster than 25 seconds, grind finer. If it takes longer than 32 seconds, grind coarser. Taste each shot. Sour, thin espresso usually means under-extraction, so grind finer or raise the temperature a degree. Bitter, dry espresso usually means over-extraction, so grind coarser or shorten the ratio.

Only change one variable per shot. If you change the grind and the dose together, you cannot tell which one fixed the problem.

Puck preparation

Distribute the grounds evenly before tamping. A few passes with a needle tool breaks up clumps and helps prevent channeling, where water finds a fast path through the puck and leaves the rest under-extracted. Tamp level. Tamping harder does not help once the bed is compressed.

Temperature

Light roasts often taste best between 93 and 95 °C. Dark roasts can taste harsh at those temperatures and often do better between 88 and 91 °C. A machine with a PID lets you hold these settings reliably.

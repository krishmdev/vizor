The Best Home Espresso Machines of 2026, Tested

We tested eight home espresso machines over six weeks, pulling more than 900 shots and logging brew temperature on every one.

Best overall: Crema Lab Aria Dual

The Aria Dual was the most complete machine we tested. Brew temperature varied by 0.6 °C across a 30-shot session, steam was strong enough to texture 300 ml of milk in 28 seconds, and the rotary pump measured 52 dB. It took 14 minutes to heat up. At $1,299 it is expensive, but nothing about it felt compromised.

Most temperature stable: Brewline Duo

The Brewline Duo posted the tightest brew temperature of the group, holding within 0.4 °C over 30 shots. It steamed 300 ml of milk in 31 seconds. The pump was louder than the Aria Dual's at 58 dB, and heat-up took 11 minutes. At $1,199 it undercuts the Aria Dual by $100.

Best for beginners: Crema Lab Aria

The Aria heated up in 5 minutes and held brew temperature within 1.1 °C. The pressure gauge and pre-infusion made it the easiest machine to learn on. Switching to steam took 50 seconds. It costs $499.

Best value: Brewline Solo

The Solo was ready in 4 minutes, the fastest of the eight, and held within 1.3 °C with its PID. It costs $449. The single boiler means a 45 second wait before steaming.

Also tested

The Kettleworks K3 ($389) had no PID and swung by 4.2 °C between shots. The Marlow Home ($749) was well built but took 22 minutes to heat up. Two machines under $300 could not hold nine bar consistently.
